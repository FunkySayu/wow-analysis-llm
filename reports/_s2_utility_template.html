The finding that reframes the rest

You cannot judge by interrupt counts. Across all 1,764 beams in this sample, not one produced a second interrupt event — the interrupt fires on the primary target only, and the area effect is a silence, which stops casts from ever starting and writes nothing to the log.

So every "interrupts landed" comparison is structurally blind to the half of the beam that matters. Measured properly — by counting enemies mid-cast inside the beam's own 8-second window — the spread runs from 3.33 casters per beam in Murder Row down to 1.40 in Kings' Rest, a factor of 2.4 that no interrupt table shows.

What your beam is doing

1,764 beams

Each beam was classified by what was casting around it. Suppressed means enemies were mid-cast in the eight seconds before and silent for the eight after — the silence worked. Contested means something was still casting inside the window: either standing outside the beam, or starting after it expired. Empty means nothing was casting on either side.

DungeonBeamsCompositionSuppressedContestedCasters/beam
Only 4% of beams across the whole sample were cast into nothing at all, so these are not wasted globals. The contested share is the interesting number: in Murder Row two thirds of beams leave casters still casting, because the imp packs are wider than one beam.

What this does not settle. A contested beam is not necessarily a mistake. The logs record no positions, so a caster counted as "still casting" may be twenty yards outside the beam and never was catchable. What the split does establish is relative: Murder Row and Altar of Fangs put two to three casters in front of every beam, while Kings' Rest and Voidscar Arena put up 1.4 and 1.5 — there, the beam is a ranged kick with a silence attached, and should be spent like one.

What it answers, and what it cannot

For every Shadowmeld, the abilities landing on that druid in the six seconds before were compared against the six seconds after. A high stop rate means dropping out of the enemy's target list ended the attack; a zero means the damage was never aimed at them in the first place.

Read the top and bottom of this table together. Targeted attacks — melee swings, channelled beams and drains — stop between 75% and 100% of the time, because losing the target ends them. Untargeted ground effects stop 0% of the time: Shadowmeld removes you from a target list, not from a puddle.

Haranir, the new option. Midnight's allied race can be a Druid, but its kit is throughput and flavour rather than utility: Lash Out (+1% critical strike damage and healing), Thorn Bloom (a 3-minute area damage-and-heal), plus out-of-combat travel and gathering passives. There is no targeting-drop equivalent, so Night Elf remains the only druid race whose racial changes how a pack is handled.

How this was measured

Reproducible

Sample. The twenty highest-keystone Balance Druid logs on Warcraft Logs for each of the eight Season 2 dungeons, de-duplicated by report and fight, keys 13 to 16. The dungeon pool itself is WCL zone 55, not a guide list.

Dispel types were derived, not looked up. No public database exposes a reliable dispel school for these debuffs. Instead every dispel of a given debuff was collected across the sample and intersected against what each dispel spell is capable of removing — can only take Curse or Poison, only Magic or Curse, and so on. Where the intersection lands on one school, that is the school.

Shapeshifts appear in the dispel log. Breaking a root by shifting is recorded as a dispel whose "dispel spell" is or . That is how the shapeshift column was counted — these are real, observed clears, not a list of things that theoretically could be shifted out of.

Two corrections worth carrying forward. Typhoon's cast is logged under spell 61391, not the tooltip's 132469; counting the latter returns zero casts in every run. And Solar Beam's interrupt is logged under 97547, separate from the 78675 cast — pairing the two is what exposed the single-interrupt ceiling.

Shadowmeld was judged by what it stopped. For each of the 237 melds, damage landing on that druid in the six seconds before was compared with the six seconds after. Two traps here: dataType: DamageTaken silently returns nothing when filtered by targetID, so each meld was queried as an unfiltered time window and filtered locally; and a first pass was wrong because a $W window constant collided with a $w events variable — PowerShell variables are case-insensitive. That bug inverted the headline, reporting most melds as idle when in fact 76% were pressed under fire.

Limits. Cast events carry no coordinates, so every "casters per beam" figure is a time-window upper bound. Counts are per run and averaged over twenty runs; a dungeon's route varies between groups, so pull-level figures describe the common route rather than a fixed one. Runs were selected by key level from the DPS rankings, so every group here contains a Balance Druid playing well — this is what good druids do, not what the average druid does.
