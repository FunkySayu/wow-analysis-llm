Coiled Isle Fight Book

Nine encounters, measured rather than guessed: where your damage actually goes, where it comes back at you, and which of your two hero trees to bring. The short version is that you are running Keeper of the Grove on two fights that do not want it.

simc 1210-01 · 12.1.0.69299 sims 12 runs · 15k iterations logs 9 encounters, WCL API v2 spells 192, live tooltips

What this concluded

Elune's Chosen wins at every target count from two upward, and leads at one. Across five builds and eleven fight shapes the hero-tree ordering never changed once. Keeper of the Grove was not the strongest build in a single scenario tested.

Your doc assigns Keeper of the Grove to Entombed Sentinels, Vashnik and Sszorak. Sszorak is defensible — it is genuinely single-target, and is the talent that lets you reach its off-grid burn window. The other two are not: Vashnik is the raid's real add fight, where the gap runs to +41,757 DPS.

Two things follow. First, at one target the entire field spans 1.5% — on a true single-target fight the build hardly matters. Second, by three targets the field spans 16.3%, and that gap is almost entirely the hero tree rather than the individual talent picks.

The same ordering holds under add waves. Six wave scenarios were run on top of a single boss — two and four adds, windows of 10 to 30 seconds, cadences of 45, 60 and 90 seconds. In all six, EC · lunation placed first and EC · goldrinn second, with the three Keeper builds third through fifth. Six independent fight shapes, one ordering, no exceptions.

What these sims do not settle. The ranking among the three Keeper builds swings by up to 17,000 DPS between wave cadences with no mechanism I can attribute it to — is best at a 60-second cadence and worst at 45 and 90, which is the opposite of what its 45-second would predict. The results reproduce to within 0.1%, so this is real behaviour of the model rather than noise, but it is not something to act on. The add-wave runs give adds a fixed lifetime rather than health, so they despawn instead of dying. The hero-tree conclusion does not rest on them — it rests on the sustained target-count table above.

So the rule you proposed holds, with one refinement. On large single-target or soak damage, Barkskin is the primary answer. On raid-wide damage it is a genuine second layer rather than a duplicate, because amplifies the incoming raid healing. The current notes mix "this wipes the raid" with "this is rot nobody reacts to", in arbitrary order. Ranking every boss's notes by consequence makes them scannable between pulls, and lets you drop anything that is both low-consequence and obvious.

TierMeaningTest for inclusion
raid wipeThe pull ends if this is missedAlways list, always first.
you dieYou personally dieAlways list.
someone diesSomeone else diesList — and flag separately when the victim is a healer or tank, since that usually cascades into a wipe.
healer CDReal intake needing a planned cooldownList if it needs assignment; otherwise leave it in the ability table.

Method and confidence

What to trust
  • Talent strings resolved, not guessed. All four export hashes in your doc were run through debug=1, which prints every allocated trait by name from the same game data the sim uses. Every label in your doc turned out to be correct — the Nek'zali "Elune Chosen?" can lose its question mark.
  • Damage numbers have two independent sources, and an earlier claim here was wrong. Guide sites publish none for this tier and the Adventure Journal ships literal X placeholders, so the first version of this report said no damage values existed anywhere. That was a mistake about where to look: Wowhead's tooltip API serves fully tuned values, and every ability tooltip on this page now carries them. Aggregate totals still come from Warcraft Logs kill data, so the two cross-check each other.
  • Cadence comes from BigWigs. The addon's boss modules carry the encounter-timeline duration constants straight from the game, split by difficulty. That is where the 111s Dig In, the 68s Twin Fangs cycle and the 85s Coiled Altar recurrences come from.
  • Two source conflicts settled by the logs. Sszorak's Ula'tek's Presence was flagged as an uncorroborated single-source claim — it is real, and the largest intake on that fight. Vashnik's Shrouded Venom count was disputed (Icy Veins said five, Method said one); the log shows four distinct instances, so neither is exactly right.
  • One agent did not finish. The log-extraction pass hit a session limit partway through building a per-15-second target-count curve, so the timelines here are cadence-derived rather than measured second by second. The damage totals it had already pulled are complete and are what this report uses.

Known weak points. Ula'tek's timings do not exist anywhere yet and are not invented here. Add counts, add health and add lifetimes are unpublished for every encounter in the raid. The add-wave sims give adds a fixed lifetime rather than health, which is the likely cause of their non-monotonic behaviour — that experiment needs redoing before its within-tree ordering means anything. Boss numbering follows the in-game journal; Blizzard's own news post uses a different order because the instance branches.
