Balance Druid in 12.1

Two fight shapes, two different answers. On a single target your build already wins. On a council pull it loses by a fifth — and in both cases the action list matters more than any talent.

What this audit concluded

The single largest gain available is replacing your action list, not changing talents: +1.3% on a patchwerk fight and +16.3% on a council pull, on your current gear. One line in that list is worth +7,209 DPS by itself.

Talents split by fight shape. Your Keeper of the Grove build is 1st of 20 at one target and 20th of 20 at three to eight. That is a hero-tree problem, not a tuning problem, and it needs a second loadout rather than a fix.

Action lists

Copy & paste ready

Paste either block into a Raidbots custom APL box or a local .simc profile, below your character import.

What the community list does that the default does not

  • Anchor-cooldown chaining — the big one. eclipse,if=prev_gcd.1.force_of_nature fires Eclipse straight off Force of Nature. Removing this one line costs 7,209 DPS (−3.2%).
  • Modelled passive Astral Power. variable.passive_asp estimates incoming Astral Power from DoT ticks, Nature’s Balance and Fury of Elune, and spends ahead of the overcap.
  • Stack management. !buff.starlord.at_max_stacks stops Starlord being wasted — 410 DPS at one target, 701 at four.
  • Burst-hold scheduling. Pooling thresholds sized in whole spender casts, banking into Celestial Alignment.
Ablation / tuning changeKeeper @ 1TElune’s Chosen @ 4T
Deltas against each archetype’s own baseline.

Talent choice changes the optimal list in exactly one place. Dropping the passive-Astral-Power model is worth +36 (nothing) on Keeper but −1,230 on Elune’s Chosen. That fits the mechanism: Elune’s Chosen generates a large, predictable, time-spread block of Astral Power from Fury of Elune, which is exactly what the model anticipates. Keeper’s generation is bursty and proc-driven, so modelling it buys nothing. Every other knob moved both archetypes the same way.

The 19 reference loadouts split into two archetypes with zero crossover, and the tooltips explain why — they are different exits from the spender cluster. Starweaver + Orbit Breaker + Meteorites is a Starfall-volume route (10/10 correlation); Rattle the Stars + Sundered Firmament is an Eclipse-entry→Fury-of-Elune route (9/9).

Hail of Stars is the highest-leverage bridge in the tree: it closes a loop from fillers → free spender → Solstice → Shooting Stars → Astral Power → more spenders. That mechanism is what produces its measured −2,533 complementary synergy with Starweaver.

Case study: Early Spring

Arithmetic, not a bug

A Druid Discord report suggested the list looks “lost in the weeds” with Early Spring, spending a plain Eclipse on the treant window that carries Convoke the Spirits. I reproduced the deficit (−2,435) and traced it — it is treant uptime, not scheduling.

BuildFoN castsDurationPredicted uptimeObserved
Early Spring (−15s cooldown)7.1110s71.1s71.9s
Bounteous Bloom (+4s duration)5.5214s77.3s77.5s
+40% duration beats +29% casts, buying 5.6 more seconds of Harmony of the Grove.

Cast counts are identical between the two builds — Celestial Alignment 4.01 vs 4.01, Convoke 5.42 vs 5.45 — so no cooldown is being dropped. Testing the scheduling fix directly:

APL variantEarly SpringBounteous

The scheduling hypothesis is not supported. Constraining Convoke to Celestial Alignment windows makes Early Spring worse by a further 3,364 — the list was already spending it well. Early Spring will not overtake Bounteous through action-list work; it needs enough cooldown reduction to flip the duration arithmetic.

Method & corrections

Reproducibility
  • Pipeline validated. Reproduced the reference sim at 221,793 against its published 221,749 — 0.02%. The reference simc revision is one commit ahead of the vendored one, and that commit is a Hunter action-list change.
  • PTR flag is a non-issue. Reference reports run ptr_enabled=1; these run live. Identical configurations differ by under 0.01% for Balance.
  • Correction carried forward. SimulationCraft enforces neither choice-node exclusivity, tier gates, connectivity, nor hero-tree membership. Four apparent gains in round one were impossible loadouts; the spec tree is exactly gate-tight, so only 120 of 485 single-point moves are legal.
  • Correction to the round-one APL claim. “No action-list change is warranted” held only for single-line edits of the default list. Replacing the list is worth an order of magnitude more.
